This report presents a stand occupancy simulation study conducted for the new terminal (T2) at Kuwait International Airport, based on actual flight movement data recorded on 16 August 2025 — the identified peak operational day of the year. The study transposes real operations recorded on that day directly onto the T2 infrastructure, providing a grounded assessment of how the new terminal would perform under current observed demand.

For each scenario, the following analytical outputs are presented: stand occupancy profiles by airline group, aircraft movement analysis, cumulative occupancy curves, heatmaps, stand duration distributions, and wide-body aircraft counts. In all scenarios, aircraft are assigned to contact stands as a priority. Under the demand levels observed on the reference day, contact stand capacity proved sufficient to accommodate all simultaneous requirements across every scenario, with no recourse to semi-contact or remote stands necessary.

The number of aircraft on stand builds progressively from the overnight baseline of 24 at 00:00, reaching its peak of 33 at 07:00 before declining as the morning wave of departures clears the apron. Starting from a minimum of 19 aircraft on stand — a level sustained for only 30 minutes — demand remains high throughout the day. Between 20 and 24 aircraft are simultaneously on stand for 11.5 hours. This rises to 25–27 aircraft for 6 hours, and to 28–32 aircraft for a further 5.5 hours.

The peak of 33 aircraft on stand is reached for only 30 minutes, confirming that the highest demand levels are short-lived. The wide-body peak of 14 aircraft occurs at 06:30, driven almost exclusively by Kuwait Airways long-haul inbound services.

Kuwait Airways has a median stand time of 138 minutes, reflecting the mix of short/medium-haul regional rotations alongside long-haul wide-body operations. Jazeera Airways has a median of 98 minutes, consistent with the high aircraft utilisation model of a low-cost carrier. Other airlines have a median of 86 minutes, concentrated in the 60–90 minute range.

A significant portion of KAC stand time represents overnight occupation rather than turnaround time — long-haul inbound flights arriving late at night remain on stand until morning departures. These overnight aircraft have been excluded from the median calculation. The morning peak is therefore not a sudden surge in arrivals, but the accumulated result of overnight parking building up from midnight onwards.

The Scenario 3 intraday profile reveals a terminal that operates at persistently low levels outside the morning peak window. The midday trough at 12:00 — just 10 aircraft — is the lowest point of the daytime period, reflecting the gap between the morning departure wave and the afternoon arrival recovery. The late evening build-up to 17 aircraft at 23:30 is driven predominantly by international network carriers completing inbound rotations before departing back to their origins in the early hours.

It is worth recalling that 16 August 2025 is the peak day of the year. On a typical operating day, these occupancy levels would be materially lower across all hours, making the underutilisation picture even more pronounced.

Findings & Conclusions
Key Conclusions
Summary of findings relevant to terminal planning and stand allocation.

The morning peak is KAC-driven and invariant. Kuwait Airways accounts for 19 aircraft on stand at the morning peak in all three configurations. This peak cannot be reduced by changing the allocation of other operators. The total peak ranges from 33 aircraft in Scenario 1 to 23 in Scenario 3, with the difference explained entirely by the presence or absence of Jazeera and the low-cost carriers.

Wide-body requirements are consistent across all scenarios. The wide-body peak of 14 aircraft in Scenarios 1 and 2, and 13 in Scenario 3, is driven almost exclusively by Kuwait Airways. Provision for at least 13 simultaneous Code E/F stands is required regardless of configuration.

Airline mix has a greater impact on daily utilisation than on peak demand. Under Scenario 1, at least 20 aircraft are on stand for nearly 24 hours per day. Under Scenario 3, that threshold is reached for only 3 hours. The removal of high-frequency short-haul operators creates extended periods of low terminal activity that cannot be compensated by full-service long-haul carriers alone.

Contact stand capacity is sufficient for peak-day demand. Under all three scenarios, peak demand can be accommodated within the terminal's contact stand positions, without recourse to semi-contact or remote stands. From a capacity perspective, the contact stand apron is more than sufficient to handle current peak-day traffic at opening.

The Scenario 3 utilisation picture is particularly acute. The terminal operates below 70% of contact stand peak capacity for more than 15 hours per day — and this is on the busiest day of the year. On an average operating day, the underutilisation would be considerably more pronounced.
